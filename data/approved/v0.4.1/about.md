# About the Research

**Context-Aware AI Safety** is an independent AI safety and algorithm research project developed by **Ryan DeJong**, an AI security engineer and researcher specializing in LLM red teaming, model safety evaluation, and applied machine learning.

Ryan's experience includes developing AI-powered cybersecurity solutions at **LOG(N) Pacific** and conducting frontier model safety evaluations through **Handshake AI**. His evaluation work has involved models such as NVIDIA Nemotron, Reflection AI Beam (501B), OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol, and GPT-6 Astra. His research also draws on volunteer work through **California State University, San Bernardino's Center for Cyber and AI**, where he has examined how AI can support legitimate cybersecurity work without enabling misuse.

## Research Purpose

The purpose of this project is to develop and evaluate methods that make AI systems safer without unnecessarily restricting legitimate users or stifling innovation. AI assistants should be capable of helping people solve complex problems without imposing excessive restrictions that undermine productivity or create unnecessary frustration. At the same time, assistance should not enable harmful activity simply because a request appears technically legitimate. This research investigates how conversational context, evidence reliability, and the potential consequences of assistance can inform more accurate safety decisions. The goal is to improve model usefulness, user satisfaction, and task completion while maintaining strong protections against harmful outcomes.

## Research Hypothesis

**Context-aware safety mechanisms can reduce unnecessary refusals without increasing harmful assistance when compared with systems that evaluate requests primarily in isolation.**

This hypothesis can be tested by comparing context-aware and prompt-only approaches on the same controlled cybersecurity evaluation cases. The primary measures would be false-refusal rates and unsafe-assistance rates. Additional measures would assess legitimate task completion, response usefulness, and user satisfaction.

## Research and Development

The current prototype demonstrates how conversational evidence can be used to evaluate AI responses and identify opportunities for improvement. It provides a foundation for developing and testing context-aware safety algorithms.

Future work will explore automated classification, model training, and response optimization. The objective is to move beyond manually analyzed examples toward methods that can improve AI behavior across a wider range of interactions.

The project remains under active development. Its proposed methods and benefits require experimental validation.
