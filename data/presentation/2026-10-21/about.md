# Overview

Context-Aware LLM Safety Classifier is an independent AI safety and algorithm research project being developed into a tool to reduce unnecessary restrictions on legitimate user requests while strengthening LLM safety and security boundaries. The current demonstration illustrates how contextual analysis, evidence-linked labeling, and proposed response improvements can inform better alignment with a user's goals without weakening safety. The goal is to support innovation without unduly restricting legitimate uses of AI, while strengthening protections against misuse of both frontier and open models.

## Demonstration

The current demonstration illustrates how multi-turn conversations and trajectories can be analyzed using contextual evidence. The displayed research cases include original conversation records, relevant context, evidence classifications, scenario backgrounds and summaries, and proposed assistant responses linked to specific turns.

The classifier under development is intended to ingest conversations and evaluate factors such as user-provided profiles, artifacts, subject-matter expertise, technical targets, authorization evidence, and other relevant contextual data. AI-assisted algorithms will classify conversation turns, passages, and branches, assign customizable labels, and generate source-grounded summaries and proposed responses aimed at minimizing harm while maximizing usefulness to the user.

Human reviewers will be able to edit classifications, refine label categories, evaluate individual user and assistant turns, and collaborate with a built-in council of LLM agents connected locally, on-premises, or through cloud APIs. The broader objective is to improve how models are evaluated and trained for safety while accounting for legitimate usefulness within different user contexts.

## Author

Ryan DeJong is an AI security engineer and researcher specializing in LLM red teaming, model safety evaluation, and applied machine learning, with over a decade of experience in compliance and GRC. Ryan's experience includes developing AI-powered cybersecurity solutions at LOG(N) Pacific and conducting frontier model safety evaluations through Handshake AI. His evaluation work has involved and contributed to pre-release training efforts for models such as NVIDIA Nemotron, Reflection AI Beam (501B), OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol, and GPT-6 Astra. His research also draws on volunteer work through California State University, San Bernardino's Center for Cyber and AI and NCyTE, examining how AI can support legitimate cybersecurity work within enterprise networks without enabling misuse.
